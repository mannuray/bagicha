# Guava care: final for owner review (2026-11-12)

Sources (full links at the end): CIB&RC Major Uses of Pesticides to 30.09.2026 (MUP); PAU Package of Practices for Fruit Crops (PAU); NHB guava pages (NHB-n = gua00n.pdf); NIPHM/DPPQS AESA-based IPM package for guava (NIPHM); NHM IPM schedule for guava pests 2012 (NHM); TNAU Agritech guava (TNAU); KAU Package of Practices 2016 (KAU). I did not reach a CISH extension document that gives months or doses, so CISH is not cited.
Page numbers: PAU, KAU and MUP use the printed page number. NIPHM and NHM use the PDF page number, because those PDFs print none.

**Label claims (they decide every chemical line).** Neither the MUP insecticide list nor the bio-insecticide list has any claim for guava. The fungicide list has two, both for fruit rot: mancozeb 75 WP (MUP-F p.19) and zineb 75 WP (MUP-F p.43). Neither entry gives a waiting period ("–"), so neither ships. This file has no pesticide line. The copper in row 3 and in the cut-sealing paste is home-made Bordeaux; see row 3's owner call.

Timing groups:
- **A = NORTH_INDIA.** Fixed dates. PAU and NHB both say North India should take the winter crop. PAU gives fixed dates to get it: cut shoot tips Apr 20–30 and hold back water Apr–May (PAU p.41; NHB-4).
- **S = SOUTH_DECCAN.** Fixed dates from TNAU: two crops, feeding in March and October, pruning Feb–Mar and Sep–Oct. These dates are Tamil Nadu sources. Karnataka, Andhra and Telangana timing is not separately verified. NHB-4 advises one crop a year, with South India preferring the rainy crop, while TNAU schedules both crops. The S rows below don't force either choice.

Rows per group: A has 5 (1–5). S has 4 (4–7). Row numbers are kept from the draft; row 8 was removed.

Month check (at most 3 reminders a month): A April has 1 and 2; A May has 2, 3 and 4. S March has 4, 6 and 7; S October has 5, 6 and 7. No month goes over 3, so the app's cap drops nothing.

**Dose** (rows 4–5 and Y1–Y3). Source NHB-6, which gives N 100, P 50 and K 50 g for each year of age, up to 5 years (the cap age). I read P and K as P₂O₅ and K₂O, as the NHB text above that table does.
- Why NHB-6: no ICAR institute (CISH) dose was reached. Of the other sources, NHB is the lowest on K, and N agrees across them (see Disagreement).
- Urea: 100 g N ÷ 0.46 = 217 g. SSP: 50 g P₂O₅ ÷ 0.16 = 313 g. MOP: 50 g K₂O ÷ 0.60 = 83 g. That is per year of age, up to 5 years.
- Bearing, two doses (rows 4–5): each dose is urea 110 g + SSP 155 g + MOP 40 g × age.
- Young, three doses (Y1–Y3): each dose is urea 72 g + SSP 104 g + MOP 28 g × age.
- Example, a 4-year-old bearing tree: urea 440 g + SSP 620 g + MOP 160 g per dose.
- **FYM is row text, not a per-age dose.** NHB-6 gives FYM only in age bands that don't grow in a straight line: 5–8, 10, 15–25, 25–30 and 40–50 kg as the tree ages, and 30–40 kg for a bearing tree. Row 4 gives the bearing figure (30–40 kg) and Y1 gives the bands, as row text.
- Disagreement (owner to note):
  - N agrees across sources: about 500 g N at full size from NHB, NIPHM p.24 and PAU p.39 (1 kg urea).
  - K does not. NHB gives 250 g K₂O, NIPHM 500 g, KAU 260 g, and PAU 1.5 kg MOP, which is 900 g K₂O.
  - TNAU gives 1 kg each of N, P and K plus 50 kg FYM for a full-grown tree, twice NHB's N.

Plant page only (never reminders):
- FYM for the year (NHB-6, by age, not per year): 5–8 kg, then 10, 15–25, 25–30 and 40–50 kg as the tree grows; 30–40 kg for a bearing tree. (NHB-6)
- Watering a young tree: in year 1 every 2–3 days, in years 2–3 every 4–5 days, in the dry months. No watering in the monsoon unless there is a long dry spell. Mulch with dry leaves or straw. (NHB-5; NHB-4 mulch)
- A, if you kept the rainy-season crop (no tip cut in April):
  - cover mature-green fruit with white non-woven bags at the end of June to mid-July;
  - hang a fruit fly trap in the first week of July;
  - pick at colour break.
  (PAU p.41–43)
- Picking: pick when the skin turns from dark green to greenish-yellow; don't leave ripe fruit on the tree. Pick up fallen fruit every day and bury it at least 60 cm deep. (PAU p.42–43; NHB-3)
- Fruit fly (any region): collect fallen and stung fruit daily and bury it 60 cm deep. Lightly dig the basin after harvest to expose the pupae. (PAU p.43; NHM p.17; NIPHM p.25)
- If you see brown, sunken spots with pink ooze on fruit in the rains (fruit rot):
  - don't let water stand in the basin;
  - bury rotten and dried-up fruit;
  - cut out dead twigs.
  (PAU p.44; NIPHM p.27)
- If leaves yellow and branches dry on one side, then the whole tree wilts (wilt): uproot and burn the tree with its roots, keep basins drained, and replant with a plant on Sardar or Portugal rootstock. (PAU p.37, p.44; NHM p.5)
- If leaves are small and yellow between the veins, with twigs dying back (zinc): spray zinc sulphate 10 g + unslaked lime 5 g per litre, 2–3 times a fortnight apart in Jun–Sep. Wear gloves; it is a fertiliser, so there is no waiting period. (PAU p.40)
- If you see silk webs with chewed wood on the trunk (bark-eating caterpillar):
  - pull off the webs;
  - push a wire into the hole;
  - plug it with cotton soaked in kerosene;
  - seal it with mud.
  (NIPHM p.26; NHM p.18)
- If the tree is near mango trees and mealybugs climb the trunk in winter: tie a 25 cm wide, 400-gauge polythene band around the trunk. (NHM p.21)
- S, if fruit is cracked or misshapen (boron): spray borax 3 g per litre at flowering and again at fruit set. Wear gloves; it is a fertiliser, so there is no waiting period. (TNAU)
- Shaping (no month in the sources):
  - clear the first 60–90 cm of trunk;
  - keep 4–5 main branches 20–25 cm apart;
  - head the tree back at 1.5–1.8 m to open the centre.
  (NHB-4; PAU p.38)

## Why the pending regions wait (research, not shipped)
- **CENTRAL_INDIA and WEST_INDIA: the bahar is the grower's choice.** The tree is stressed to flower once a year, in one of three seasons (NHB-4):

  | Bahar | Water stress | Flowering | Fruit |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Ambe | Dec–Jan | Feb–Mar | Jul–Aug |
  | Hasta | Aug–Sep | Oct–Nov | Mar–Apr |
  | Mrig | from 3rd week of April | Jun–Jul | Nov–Dec |

  Root pruning is only for heavy soils; in light soils, holding back water does the job (NHB-4). Feeding follows the bahar, not the calendar. When irrigation is cut, apply FYM + half the N + all the P and K. Give the other half of the N 1–1.5 months after fruit set (NHB-6). These become FROM_CHOICE rows once per-tree timing exists:
  - stress start (by bahar);
  - feed at the end of the stress;
  - second N 30–45 days after fruit set;
  - fruit-fly hygiene during the fruit months.

  I found no source that names one bahar as the home-garden standard for MP, Vidarbha, Gujarat, Rajasthan or western Maharashtra.
- **EAST_INDIA: the crop choice is unsourced.** Bihar guava flowers twice, in spring and in the rains (secondary sources). No BAU Sabour or BCKV recommendation turned up that fixes a winter crop.
- **COASTAL_HUMID: no dated source.** KAU gives a full-grown dose (80 kg FYM, 200 g N, 80 g P₂O₅, 260 g K₂O) "in two or three split doses when there is sufficient moisture", with no months (KAU p.215). TNAU's dates are for the drier Tamil Nadu plains.

## Removed
- **Removed as unverified (final pass):**
  - row 8, "Pick fruit firm and greenish-yellow", Jan 1–Feb 28: the window was computed from PAU's Aug–Sep flowering plus NHB-3's "about 5 months to ripen". The picking advice itself moves to the plant page with no dates;
  - the A harvest phase (Jan–Feb), for the same reason;
  - Y4, "Water the young tree": the dry-month windows were copied from the approved mango table. The intervals stay on the plant page;
  - FYM 8 kg per year of age: fitted to NHB-6's bands, not quoted;
  - every row and phase column for EAST_INDIA, CENTRAL_INDIA, WEST_INDIA and COASTAL_HUMID (now pending).
- **Removed: no waiting period:** mancozeb 75 WP 2 g per litre for fruit rot (MUP-F p.19 gives none for guava).
- **Not approved (no guava label claim in MUP 30.09.2026):**
  - copper oxychloride or Blitox (PAU, NHM, NHB, TNAU);
  - cuprous oxide;
  - captafol or Difolatan, which is banned;
  - carbendazim or Bavistin;
  - Brassicol;
  - chlorothalonil;
  - wettable sulphur;
  - quinalphos, malathion, fenthion, monocrotophos, dimethoate, triazophos, phosalone, carbaryl and ethofenprox;
  - buprofezin, dichlorvos, chlorpyriphos and phorate (Thimet);
  - the malathion in methyl-eugenol bottle traps;
  - neem oil + soap spray (no azadirachtin claim for guava);
  - formalin pit drench.
- **Not used:**
  - zineb 75 WP (it has a claim but no waiting period);
  - the NAA, 2,4-D, DNOC and GA3 crop-regulation and fruit-set sprays (I did not check the growth-regulator list);
  - the urea 10% deblossoming spray (pruning in row 1 does the same job).
- **Unverified from the draft:**
  - frost care and flower removal for young trees;
  - a CISH dose (only a trial figure turned up: 10 kg FYM + 120:60:50 g NPK per year of age);
  - Karnataka, AP and Telangana timing as separate from Tamil Nadu.
- **Owner decision:** TNAU's red-rust spray (home-made Bordeaux 0.6%) was dropped. South Deccan trees carry fruit most of the year, and no waiting period is sourced. Home-made Bordeaux is kept only for row 3 (no fruit on the tree; OWNER CALL) and as a paste for cuts, following the mango precedent.
- **Not routine:** rejuvenating old trees (head back to 1.5 m in March, PAU p.38); budding and nursery practice.
