# Apple care: draft + source check (2026-11-23)

Apple is a hill tree. It needs 800–1,600 hours of winter chill (CITH p.5), flowers once in spring and is picked once in late summer or autumn. So most of its care follows the calendar:
- winter pruning;
- the winter manure feed and two nitrogen feeds;
- summer watering;
- thinning;
- picking;
- the autumn leaf clean-up.

What the gardener has to see first (scab, mildew, leaf fall, canker, woolly aphid, scale, root borer, mites, collar rot) is `conditional`: plant page only. The commercial 7–8-spray scab calendar (CITH p.34; SKUAST-J pp.74–75; NHB app002) is kept out of reminders, as the owner asked.

Kinds: none. The catalogue has:
- the plain entry, Royal Delicious, Golden Delicious and Ambri, all `hills_cool`;
- HRMN-99 (low-chill), listed for `north_india`, `east_india`, `central_india`, `west_india` and `south_deccan`. Its catalogue note reads: "Regions from DST/ICAR adoption reports, not a university recommendation."

`grownIn` = HILLS_COOL, NORTH_INDIA, EAST_INDIA, CENTRAL_INDIA, WEST_INDIA, SOUTH_DECCAN. `pendingRegions` = the five plains regions (§1, Q1).

## 2. Rules used

1. **Placement of manure and fertiliser:**
   - Spread in the tree basin from 30 cm away from the trunk out to the drip line, and mix it into the soil (NIPHM p.23).
   - Water after the winter feed (NHB app006; CITH p.23).
   - Wear gloves.
2. **Doses per year of age, as the guides give them** (NIPHM pp.22–23):
   - N : P₂O₅ : K₂O = **70 : 35 : 70 g per year of age**, stabilised at 700 : 350 : 700 g after 10 years. NIPHM's own pre-planting line says 70 : 30 : 70 (Q3).
   - FYM **10 kg per year of age, up to 100 kg**.
   - Converted at fixed nutrient contents: urea 46% N, single superphosphate (SSP) 16% P₂O₅, muriate of potash (MOP) 60% K₂O.
     - 35 g N → **76 g urea**;
     - 35 g P₂O₅ → **219 g SSP**;
     - 70 g K₂O → **117 g MOP**.
   - The conversion is arithmetic, shown so the owner can check it (Q3).
   - Banded tables (SKUAST-J, CITH, PPQS, NHB) are shown as conflicts in §3D; they are never turned into a per-year rule.
3. **Bordeaux (owner exception, 2026-11-12): paste on cuts only.**
   - SKUAST-J (p.71) says "large cuts should be covered with Bordeaux paste". It also prescribes Bordeaux paste on cleaned cankers (p.77) and on a cleaned collar-rot wound (p.76).
   - The apple guides give no recipe, so it is taken from **PAU p.10**: 2 kg copper sulphate dissolved in 15 L water; 3 kg quicklime slaked in another 15 L; mix the two.
   - For a home garden, at the same ratio: dissolve **100 g copper sulphate in 750 ml water**; slake **150 g quicklime in 750 ml water**; mix the two and brush the paste on the cut. Use a plastic or clay pot, never iron. Wear gloves.
   - **No apple guide reached recommends a 1% Bordeaux dormant spray or a Bordeaux soil drench**, so neither is in this draft (Q6).
4. **Fertilisers (urea, SSP, MOP) and the 5% urea leaf spray** have no waiting period.
5. **Chemicals:** only products with an apple row in MUP that gives a waiting period. The dose per litre comes from the label's water volume (§4).

Stage anchors used for months (SKUAST-J p.75, Jammu temperate hills):
- silver tip to green tip: mid-March to mid-April;
- pink bud: April;
- petal fall: late April to end of May;

## 6. Open questions for the owner
- **Q1. HRMN-99 in the plains** (NORTH, EAST, CENTRAL, WEST, SOUTH_DECCAN): no credible ICAR, SAU or state source gives care for low-chill apple there. The catalogue's own note says its regions come from adoption reports.
  - Recommendation: **keep all five in `pendingRegions`**, so plains gardeners keep the season-pack advice.
  - Revisit if an SAU package appears (e.g. CCSHAU, UHS Bagalkot, IGKV or an ICAR low-chill trial with months).
  - Separately, the owner may want to reconsider listing HRMN-99 in five plains regions at all.
- **Q2. One calendar for all of HILLS_COOL.** The months come from J&K, HP and Uttarakhand between about 1,500 and 2,500 m. Lower orchards flower and pick weeks earlier, and Kashmir's snow can delay pruning. No source was read for Sikkim, Darjeeling or the north-east hill states, which the app also maps to HILLS_COOL.
  - Recommendation: **ship it as written**. The windows are wide (pruning Dec–Jan, harvest Aug–Oct), and the harvest text tells the gardener to judge by seed colour.
  - Add a north-east source later. Until then, accept that NE apple gardeners get the north-west calendar.
- **Q3. Feed dose.** NIPHM's per-year rule (70 : 35 : 70 g NPK and 10 kg FYM per year of age, capped at 10 years) is used, converted to urea 76 g × age per half, SSP 219 g × age and MOP 117 g × age.
  - It is the highest of the five sources. At 10 years it gives 700 g N, against about 300 g in SKUAST-J's table, 600 g at 15 years in CITH and a flat 350 g in NHB.
  - NIPHM's own pre-planting line says 30 g P₂O₅, not 35.
  - An HP table seen only in a search summary (280 g CAN, 220 g SSP, 120 g MOP and 10 kg FYM per year) looks the same, but it was **not fetched or verified** and is not counted.
  - Recommendation: **accept NIPHM with the 10-year cap.** It is the only per-year rule, from a national guide written with Dr YSP UHF scientists.
  - If the owner prefers caution, halve the N (SKUAST-J level) by capping the urea at 5 years (380 g per half).
  - CAN (280 g × age a year) can be offered as the nitrogen source instead of urea for acid hill soils, if wanted.
- **Q4. Young trees on the same calendar.** NIPHM dates the winter feed "in bearing trees" and gives the per-year rule from year 1, with no separate month for young trees. Recommendation: **apply H2, H4 and H5 to young trees too**. The per-year dose keeps a 1–2-year tree's share small (10–20 kg FYM, 76–152 g urea per half).
- **Q5. Chaubattia paste** (red lead + copper carbonate + linseed oil: CITH p.32; also named by SKUAST-J). Recommendation: **leave it out.** Red lead is toxic for a home garden, and SKUAST-J names Bordeaux paste as the alternative for the same cuts and cankers.
- **Q6. Bordeaux 1% dormant spray and stem-base drench.** The owner's exception allows both, but no apple guide reached recommends either. The guides use copper only as paste on cuts, cankers and collar-rot wounds. Recommendation: **don't add them** until an apple source (e.g. the HP Horticulture Department spray schedule) gives one. The pomegranate recipe should not be imported.
- **Q7. Lime sulphur without a waiting period.** The MUP row gives no waiting days. Its active-ingredient column (0.22%) matches a 1% rate (10 ml/L). The "2% pre & 1% post blossom" figures come from the dilution column, and NIPHM p.23 restates them. Recommendation:
  - **keep it, limited to the bare tree before flowers open (20 ml per litre)**. Fruit is months away, so no waiting period applies;
  - leave out the 1% post-blossom use, which would fall on fruit with no stated wait.
- **Q8. Phosmet for woolly aphid.** It is labelled (31-day wait), but the dose cells are garbled for a powder; they read most plausibly as 1 g per litre. Recommendation: **leave it out** and ship C5 with the organic steps. Revisit if the CIB&RC label or a guide confirms the per-litre dose.
- **Q9. Growth regulators.** Hydrogen cyanamide (2%, four weeks before bloom, 188-day wait), GA₃ and paclobutrazol carry apple claims. CITH and SKUAST-J recommend 3% Dormex, which is above the label. Recommendation: **leave all out**. They are commercial yield tools and hazardous to handle; a home tree in the hills gets its chill naturally.
- **Q10. Scab and mildew products.** Kresoxim-methyl (7 days) is used for scab and bupirimate (15 days) for mildew; both are newer and may be harder to find in small packs. Recommendation: keep them. If the owner prefers an older product, dodine 65 WP (0.75 g/L, 21 days) also covers scab, and it is the same family as C3's leaf-fall product.
