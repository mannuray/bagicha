# Amla care: final for owner review (2026-11-12)

Amla is also called aonla.

## What changed in the final pass
- CENTRAL_INDIA and EAST_INDIA are now pending. They were inferred copies of the North windows, with no source of their own. Only North (A) ships, and the sameAs links are gone.
- Dose: CISH (the ICAR institute) is used: 5 kg FYM per year of age, not NHB's 10 kg. The arithmetic is shown below and is unchanged from the draft.
- "Wear gloves" added to the borax spray (row 7) and to the alkaline-soil nutrient dressing.
- No row in the North table was derived, borrowed or marked "(check)", so no North rows were removed.

**MUP 30.09.2026: no label claim for aonla / amla / Indian gooseberry** in any of the insecticide, fungicide or biopesticide lists. So there are **no chemical alternatives** in this file, and no sulphur, copper, mancozeb or neem products either: these are all registered pesticides. Every pest and disease line is cultural or mechanical.

Groups: **A = NORTH_INDIA** only.

Pending regions (keep today's advice): CENTRAL_INDIA and EAST_INDIA (were inferred copies of North); WEST_INDIA, SOUTH_DECCAN, COASTAL_HUMID (no dated amla source). HILLS_COOL is not listed as a growing region, as for mango.

Why A: amla is a subtropical, calendar-driven tree. It drops leaves in winter, flowers mid-March to April, holds its fruitlets dormant through May–June, swells them with the monsoon and ripens Oct–Dec. The CISH calendar is written for the subtropical belt (UP). The NHB report gives the same months and names no region.
No Central-India (JNKVV/IGKV) or East-India (BAU Sabour/RPCAU) aonla package was found, so those regions are pending rather than copies of the North.

NEEDS PER-TREE TIMING: **none.** Dormancy, flowering, feeding and harvest all follow the calendar. No source describes a grower-regulated crop.

| 5 | Give the second feed | Jul 15–Aug 31 | — | Other half of the urea + other half of the MOP in the ring; water in if there is no rain | Feeds fruit growth. CISH says July, NHB says August | CISH p.22; NHB §5.6 |

Rows: bearing 8, young 4 (A only). Month check (at most 3 a month): bearing January has 1 and 2; September has 6 and 7; October has 7 and 8. Young: January and February have Y1 and Y3; March and April have Y2 and Y4. Nothing passes 2, so the app's cap drops nothing.

Dose (rows 2 and 5, and Y1): per year of age, up to 10 years (the cap age).
Rule: ICAR institute first. CISH (ICAR) is used for every figure. Where NHB or NIPHM differ, they are noted below and not used.
CISH p.20 gives N 100 g, P 50 g, K 100 g and FYM 5 kg per year of age, reaching 1 kg N, 0.5 kg P, 1 kg K and 50 kg FYM at 10 years. NHB §5.6 gives the same N, P and K. "P" and "K" are read as P2O5 and K2O, the usual package-of-practice convention, as in mango.
- Per year of age for the whole year: urea 100 ÷ 0.46 = 217 g; SSP 50 ÷ 0.16 = 313 g; MOP 100 ÷ 0.60 = 167 g.
- Split (NHB §5.6, which accounts for every nutrient; CISH's January line leaves half the P and K unassigned):
  - **Row 2 (Jan–Feb):** FYM 5 kg + urea 110 g + SSP 310 g + MOP 85 g, × age.
  - **Row 5 (Jul–Aug):** urea 110 g + MOP 85 g, × age.
- Check at 10 years: urea 2.2 kg × 0.46 = 1.01 kg N ✓; SSP 3.1 kg × 0.16 = 0.50 kg P2O5 ✓; MOP 1.7 kg × 0.60 = 1.02 kg K2O ✓; FYM 50 kg ✓.
- Example, a 6-year-old: Jan 30 kg FYM + 660 g urea + 1.86 kg SSP + 510 g MOP; Jul–Aug 660 g urea + 510 g MOP.

Conflicts, not used:
- NHB gives 10 kg FYM per year of age. CISH's 5 kg is used (ICAR first; it is also the lower dose).
- NIPHM p.22 gives 30 g N per year of age, split Sep–Oct and Apr–May. It is lower than CISH, but NIPHM (DPPQS) is not the ICAR institute, so CISH stands. Owner to note.
- TNAU gives a flat bearing dose (10 kg FYM, 200 g N, 500 g P, 200 g K) for Tamil Nadu.

Plant page only (never reminders):
- If you see silk-and-sawdust webbing on bark with holes at branch joints (bark-eating caterpillar, from April): scrape off the webbing, push a wire into each hole to kill the caterpillar, put a few drops of kerosene in the hole and seal it with wet mud. (NHM p.10; NIPHM p.23; CISH p.21; TNAU)
- If shoot tips swell into galls (seen Oct–Nov): cut the galled shoots after harvest and burn them; don't let branches crowd. (NHM p.10–11; NIPHM p.23)
- If new shoots twist with white mealybugs or aphids and ants on them: clip off and destroy the infested shoots early. (NHM p.12–13; CISH p.21; NIPHM p.23–24)
- If leaves are webbed together with a small caterpillar inside (leaf roller): clip the rolled leaves at the start and destroy them. (NHM p.13–14; CISH p.22)
- If young fruits have bore holes with frass (pomegranate butterfly, Sep–Oct): pick and destroy them. Don't grow pomegranate or guava right next to amla. (NHM p.11–12; NIPHM p.24)
- If you see reddish-brown pustules in rings on fruit or leaves (rust, Jul–Sep): cut out badly infected shoots and keep the canopy open. Banarasi and Chakaiya are fairly free of it. No fungicide has an aonla label claim. (NIPHM p.25; NHM p.1)
- If branches split under a heavy crop (Lakshmi-52, NA-6, NA-7): prop them in September. (CISH p.23)
- If a lone tree flowers but sets little fruit: amla needs a second variety nearby for cross-pollination. (CISH p.22)
- If the soil is alkaline (usar/sodic): mix borax, zinc sulphate and copper sulphate, 100 g each, into the January fertiliser ring, scaled to tree age and vigour; wear gloves. This is a soil nutrient dressing, not a spray. (CISH p.20; NHB §5.6)

## Phases (bearing)
| Phase | A | Avoid | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| pre_flowering | Jan–Feb | Leaving dead and galled wood on the tree | Frost on young trees |
| flowering | Mar–Apr | Watering bearing trees; a wet basin | Mealybug and aphids on new shoots |
| fruit_dormancy | May–Jun | Letting the basin go bone dry | Bark-eating caterpillar, leaf roller, fruitlet drop |
| fruit_development | Jul–Sep | Waterlogging round the trunk | Rust, stone borer (pea-size fruit), pomegranate butterfly, shoot gall |
| harvest | Oct–Dec | Leaving ripe fruit on the tree; bruising or dropping fruit | Fruit midge, blue mould on bruised fruit |

Phase sources: CISH p.20–23; NHB §5.7; NHM p.4–15.

## Removed as not approved (no aonla label claim, MUP 30.09.2026)
- Wettable sulphur (rust, sooty mould).
- Mancozeb / Dithane M-45 and Z-78, carbendazim, thiophanate methyl, captafol (Difolatan), hexaconazole and chlorothalonil (rust, fruit rots, blue mould).
- Copper oxychloride on pruning cuts (CISH p.20–21).
- Copper sulphate + zinc sulphate + borax foliar mix (CISH p.23): copper sulphate sprayed on the tree is a fungicide use (registered as Copper Sulphate 2.62% SC, MUP fungicides p.9), with no aonla claim. Borax alone is kept.
- Dichlorvos, chlorpyriphos, monocrotophos, quinalphos, carbaryl, dimethoate, spinosad, lambda-cyhalothrin and malathion. Endosulfan is also banned in India.
- NAA, GA3 and thiourea sprays against fruit drop (CISH p.22–23): plant growth regulators with no aonla claim.
