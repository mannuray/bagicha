# Plum care: draft + source check (2026-11-23)

Plum (aloo bukhara) is a deciduous stone fruit. It bears on spurs that live 5–6 years and on one-year-old wood, so it needs a lighter yearly prune than peach (PAU p.103; UHF p.45). Every calendar row below follows a month given by a regional guide:
- winter pruning and training;
- the December manure;
- summer watering;
- the fruit-fly trap and picking in Punjab.

The urea split is tied to flowering, so it is `conditional`, as are aphids, borers, gummosis, beetles, scale and the zinc and boron sprays (plant page, never reminders).

Kinds: none. The catalogue has the plain entry (HILLS_COOL, NORTH_INDIA), Satluj Purple and Kala Amritsari (NORTH_INDIA, PAU; Satluj Purple sets fruit only with Kala Amritsari nearby) and Santa Rosa (HILLS_COOL). `grownIn` = NORTH_INDIA, HILLS_COOL.

Sources (full links at the end):
- **PAU** = Package of Practices for Cultivation of Fruits. Plum is on printed pp.101–107. Its plant protection is "as given under peach" (pp.85–87). The Bordeaux paste and paint recipes are on p.10. Printed pages.
- **UHF** = *Cultivation practices for stone fruits & walnut*, by Dr YS Parmar UHF Nauni scientists for the HP Horticulture Development Project (World Bank), hosted by HP Horticulture (eudyan). Plum and prune are on pp.37–48; diseases on pp.62–66; pests on pp.68–69. It covers Japanese plums in the mid hills (800–1500 m, p.38). Printed pages.
- **HP-SS** = *Spray Schedule 2022 (Peach, Plum, Apricot & Almond)*, Dept of Horticulture HP with Dr YSPUHF Nauni.
- **HP Shiva** = *Package of Practices for Subtropical Fruit Crops of HP* (Dr YSPUHF, COHF Neri, 2022). Plum is on pp.106–116. It covers the low and mid-low hills (Hamirpur, Una, Bilaspur and lower Kangra), all HILLS_COOL in the app. Used for backing and conflicts (Q3).
- **SKUAST** = SKUAST-Jammu, *Package of Practices for Fruit Crops* (2020). Plum is on pp.108–111; peach on pp.102–107. The temperate belt is Poonch, Rajouri, Doda (HILLS_COOL); the subtropical belt is Jammu, Samba and Kathua (NORTH_INDIA).
- **MUP** = CIB&RC Major Uses of Pesticides to 30.09.2026 (MUP-I, MUP-F, MUP-PGR). PDF pages. MUP-I has **no plum row**. MUP-F has one: lime sulphur, plum black spot. The bio-insecticide and bio-fungicide lists have no plum entry: both files were read in full.

**Not reached:** ICAR-CITH Srinagar (`cith.icar.gov.in` did not answer); GBPUAT Pantnagar (no plum package found online); Dr YSPUHF's own "Package of Practices for Fruit Crops" (not found online; UHF, HP-SS and HP Shiva, all by its scientists, stand in).

## 2. Rules used

1. **Placement:** spread manure and fertiliser in the tree basin, about 30 cm from the trunk, and mix it in lightly (UHF p.26). Water after. Wear gloves.
2. **Doses** go in `dose` only where the guide's table is an exact per-year rule (PAU plum, UHF N, P and K). Base + increment or banded figures are row text.
3. **Bordeaux (owner exception, 2026-11-12):** paste or paint on cuts and wounds only. No guide names a Bordeaux spray for plum.
   - **Bordeaux paste** (PAU p.10, scaled down 1:40):
     - Dissolve 50 g copper sulphate in 375 ml water.
     - In a second pot, slake 75 g quicklime slowly with a little of another 375 ml of water, then add the rest.
     - Mix the two and stir. Brush it on the cut.
     - Use a plastic or clay pot, never iron. Wear gloves.
   - **Bordeaux paint** (PAU p.10, scaled down 1:20):
     - Heat copper sulphate crystals (about 70 g) on an iron tawa until they crumble into a white powder (monohydrate). Weigh out 50 g of the powder.
     - Mix it well with 100 g hydrated lime dust, then stir in 150 ml boiled linseed oil until smooth.
     - Brush it on. It keeps in a closed pot. Wear gloves.
4. **Fertilisers and micronutrients** (urea, SSP, MOP, zinc sulphate, boric acid, potassium nitrate) have no waiting period.
5. **Chemicals:** only rows with a plum claim in MUP, with a per-litre dose and waiting days (§4). None survives.

**Removed lines with no replacement:**

- **Brown rot:** HP-SS's May spray (captan, mancozeb or propineb) covers plum, but none has a plum claim, and no guide gives a non-chemical step. There is no row; picking ripe fruit on time (N7, C10) helps.

## 5. What the check changed
1. **No chemical survives.** MUP has no plum insecticide row. Its only plum fungicide (lime sulphur) has no per-litre dose.
2. **The urea split is conditional** (C1). A calendar version derived from PAU's mulch timing is held (N3, N4; Q2).
3. **PAU's banded table read as a per-year rule.** Every band end matches 6 kg / 60 / 95 / 60 g × age, capped at 6 years (Q1).
4. **Mashobra paint and paste removed** (streptocycline). Bordeaux paste goes on the scraped canker (C6).
5. **Growth regulators removed:** NAA and ethrel for pre-harvest drop, and NAA for thinning (Q5).
6. **Malathion bait spray** replaced by a methyl eugenol trap and sanitation (C10).
7. **HP Shiva's low-hill calendar is not merged** with UHF's mid-hill one (Q3).
8. **Neem oil and neem cake removed** (no plum claim; citrus, papaya and pomegranate precedent).

## 6. Open questions for the owner
- **Q1. PAU's plum table read as a per-year rule** (FYM 6 kg, SSP 95 g, MOP 60 g and urea 60 g × age, capped at 6 years). Every band end matches exactly.
  - Recommendation: **accept it as `dose`**. It is PAU's own arithmetic, not a fit.
- **Q2. A calendar date for NORTH's urea split.** PAU says "before flowering, then a month later after fruit set". Its mulch line ("first week of March after … second split doses") puts the second half by early March. That suggests about Feb 1–14 and Mar 1–7.
  - Recommendation: **ship C1 (conditional) now; keep N3 and N4 held.** The dates are inferred, not stated. Revisit if a Punjab KVK gives months.
- **Q3. HILLS_COOL low hills against mid hills.** HP Shiva (subtropical HP, also HILLS_COOL in the app) differs from UHF:
  - N 50, P 25 and K 40 g × age to 5 years;
  - FYM 10 / 10 / 15 / 20 kg, with half the P, before mid-February;
  - watering every other day in March–May, none from June to January;
  - picking from the second half of May.
  - Recommendation: **use UHF**, which covers the catalogue's hill plum (Santa Rosa, mid hills). Note that low-hill gardeners (Una, Hamirpur, Bilaspur) get a mid-hill calendar. Consider splitting HILLS by altitude later.
- **Q4. UHF's 7-year cap** (500 g N against 490 g from the rule).
  - Recommendation: **use the per-year rule, maxAge 7**, as in peach Q3.
- **Q5. Growth regulators:** PAU's NAA (10 ppm) or ethrel (100 ppm) for Satluj Purple's pre-harvest drop, UHF's NAA thinning, and any chilling breaker (hydrogen cyanamide: grapes only) have no plum claim.
  - Recommendation: **leave them out**. The low-chill varieties need none in Punjab (PAU p.101: under 300 chilling hours).
- **Q6. Lime sulphur 22% SC** is labelled for plum black spot as a delayed-dormant spray. The row gives 2–5 L per hectare with no water volume, so there is no per-litre dose, and no Indian plum guide here recommends it.
  - Recommendation: **leave it out** (pomegranate quinalphos precedent: dose per litre unclear).
- **Q7. HILLS pruning window (H2, Dec 1–Feb 29).** UHF describes "first winter" and "dormant" pruning but gives no month. The window borrows UHF's dormant planting season ("December to February", p.42). PAU (Jan) and SKUAST (peach, early Jan) are not hills-plum sources.
  - Recommendation: **accept the dormant-season window**. It names no new month, only the months UHF calls dormant, and it runs while the tree is bare.
